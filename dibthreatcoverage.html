RFI Reference Companion · Threat coverage under machine-verifiable compliance

The DIB's top 10 threats — and how much of each becomes machine-verifiable under this approach

The approach evaluated: harden every in-scope system to the NIST National Checklist Program baselines, enforce the DoW's published ODP timelines and thresholds — defaulting to NIST checklist recommendations where the DoW has not set a value — and accept machine-readable evidence for that entire set. Only a handful of administrative and documentation processes are left to human review. The color on each threat is how comprehensively that evidence set covers it.

● Comprehensive ◑ Partial ○ Limited
●3 comprehensive ◑5 partial ○2 limited

Read the two "limited" rows as the feature, not the bug: supply-chain trust and business-email fraud are exactly the human-judgment residual this approach deliberately leaves to reviewers — not gaps it pretends to close.

# Threat to the DIB  ·  coverage What provides the coverage (inputs) Limitations & residual
1

Exploitation of unpatched internet-facing systems

VPNs, email/Exchange, edge devices. Vuln exploitation +34% YoY — now the top entry point for critical infrastructure (DBIR 2025). DIB-VDP found 401 real vulns across 41 firms.

● Comprehensive 		SCAP configuration conformance (attack-surface reduction) plus the ODP patch clock — 30/90/180 days by severity (03.14.01) — plus monthly authenticated scanning (03.11.02.a) and 24-hour signature currency, all reported with CVE/CVSS and finding age. Measures adherence to the published remediation clock, not immunity during the pre-patch window of a true zero-day. Coverage is only as complete as the scanned boundary.
2

Stolen or abused credentials

Credential abuse = 22% of breaches — the single top initial vector overall (DBIR 2025). "Access is the point of failure."

● Comprehensive 		Baseline enforces MFA-capable configuration, disables legacy/basic auth (the real MFA-bypass), account-lockout and minimum-password-length ODP thresholds, and Credential Guard — all readable from IdP and host config state. Configuration cannot force MFA enrollment or IdP deployment (an architecture choice → human review), and cannot detect a stolen but valid session. Phishing-resistant vs. weak MFA is a design decision.
3

Spearphishing & social engineering

Human element in ~60% of breaches; social engineering = 22% of manufacturing breaches (DBIR 2025).

◑ Partial 		Payload-execution hardening is verifiable from the baseline: internet macros blocked, Attack-Surface-Reduction rules, PowerShell logging, Windows Script Host disabled. Training completion + curriculum carried as referenced (Tier 2) evidence. Cannot observe whether a user clicks, or whether training content is adequate — both are human / artifact-quality judgments. Email-gateway efficacy is out of the host-evidence scope.
4

Ransomware

Present in 44% of breaches (+37%) — but 88% of small-business breaches, and the DIB is ~200,000 mostly-SMB firms (DBIR 2025; DC3).

◑ Partial 		Lateral-movement containment is verifiable from the baseline: SMBv1 off, SMB signing, LAPS (unique local-admin), RDP restriction, anti-ransomware ASR — reinforced by the patch and credential layers upstream. The outcome hinges on initial access (human) and on backup/recovery adequacy (contingency planning), which conformance evidence only partly reaches.
5

Supply-chain / third-party compromise

Third-party involvement doubled to 30% of breaches (DBIR 2025). SolarWinds is the defense-sector archetype.

○ Limited 		Component inventory currency, declared-vs-observed boundary reconciliation, FedRAMP / CMVP inheritance status, external-service-provider declaration (03.16.03), and the deviation register. A signed, malicious update from a trusted vendor executes with legitimate trust. Detection is behavioral and boundary-level (Tier 3), not configuration conformance.
6

Web shells for persistence

17 China Chopper web shells planted on a DIB organization's Exchange server (CISA AA22-277A).

◑ Partial 		Web/Exchange STIG conformance (least functionality, restricted script execution, constrained service-account privilege), the ODP patch clock for the entry vulnerability, and audit-log configuration + weekly review (03.03.05). Detecting an already-planted shell is a monitoring/hunt function, not conformance. Coverage depends on the upstream vulnerability being patched within the clock.
7

Nation-state RATs & custom exfiltration

HyperBro, PlugX, CovalentStealer used to steal CUI (CISA AA22-277A). Espionage motive in manufacturing breaches jumped to 20% from 3% (DBIR 2025).

◑ Partial 		Application allowlisting (WDAC / AppLocker) config, driver-signing enforcement, egress/boundary configuration, hardened persistence mechanisms, and audit logging — all part of the evidence set. A determined actor with valid credentials and signed tooling evades configuration. Exfiltration detection is a boundary / DLP + Tier 3 function, not host conformance.
8

Living-off-the-land / legitimate-tool abuse

Impacket (wmiexec, smbexec), WMI, and command shell used for lateral movement (CISA AA22-277A).

◑ Partial 		Baseline restricts remote WMI/WinRM, LAPS breaks the pass-the-hash these tools rely on, PowerShell script-block logging and a tiered-admin model are enforced — and abuse becomes an audited event. Legitimate tools used by valid accounts still require behavioral detection. Config proves the constraints exist, not that no misuse occurred.
9

Misconfiguration & exposed services

Default creds, open management planes, RDP without NLA. The definitional gap — and a large share of what DIB-VDP researchers actually found.

● Comprehensive 		SCAP/XCCDF conformance against the NCP baseline (DoW ODP where set, defaulting to NIST checklist recommendations). This threat is the absence of the baseline, so conformance measures it directly. Only as complete as the declared boundary — a host left out of scope is not checked. That gap is the Tier 3 boundary-validation residual.
10

Business email compromise / pretexting

The financially-motivated subset of social engineering; a meaningful slice of DBIR social-engineering cases.

○ Limited 		Email-authentication configuration (DMARC / SPF / DKIM) and MFA-on-email where in scope, plus training completion records. Fundamentally an identity / human / business-process attack — not addressed by host baselines or 800-171 configuration controls, and largely outside the machine-evidence scope.

How to read the coverage rating. These are qualitative security-engineering assessments of the machine-verifiable evidence set (baseline conformance + ODP timelines/thresholds + registry queries) against each vector — not measured efficacy percentages. Every rating assumes the baseline is enforced and current and the assessment boundary is complete; both assumptions are exactly what continuous reporting verifies and what the Tier 3 boundary review protects.

Data caveats. Threat figures are the Verizon 2025 DBIR (a 2026 edition now exists — lock final numbers before submission) and use manufacturing / critical infrastructure as proxies for the DIB, which the DBIR does not isolate. DIB-specific hard data is DC3's DIB-VDP and CISA advisory AA22-277A. ODP values are from the DoD CIO memo signed 10 April 2025.
