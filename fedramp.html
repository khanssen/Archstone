FedRAMP 20x · automation and readiness

20x gets you authorized. We get you relied on.

FedRAMP 20x made authorization faster. It has not made agencies rely on what it authorizes: most 20x authorizations have no agency customer, and the program's own marketplace shows it. We build the automation that gets you through 20x, and the evidence an agency needs to see before it signs.

Talk to us about 20x See the 20x adoption data

  • Key Security Indicator pipelines

    Automated collection and validation for your KSIs, built on tools you already run and kept in your own environment. You own the pipeline and the evidence, not a vendor.

  • Validation that checks scope

    A scan that ran is not a scan that covered your system. We test what each check examined and what threshold it applied, so your evidence holds up when a reviewer reads it closely.

  • Rule-level CR26 tracking

    We follow the FedRAMP Consolidated Rules change by change and map each one to what you have to produce, so a rule change does not turn into a finding.

  • Packages agencies will rely on

    Authorization is the start. We plan for what an authorizing official needs to see before relying on a 20x package, and build it into your evidence from day one.

What stays with the agency

A FedRAMP certification is not an agency's authority to operate. FedRAMP's 2026 rules adopted the word "certification" in part to keep the two apart. The agency still authorizes its own use of your service under FISMA, and still owns the controls your service does not cover: how its users are provisioned, how its data is configured and protected, and what it monitors.

We make those responsibilities explicit in your package and give the agency the evidence it needs to meet them. A service whose customer responsibilities are clear is easier to authorize, and easier to keep relying on.

Traditional FedRAMP

  • Boundary definition: the components, interfaces and data flows that fall inside your authorization boundary.
  • Documentation: system security plans, risk assessments, contingency plans and the rest of the package.
  • Gap analysis: where your system falls short of the baseline, and what to do about it.
  • Policies, procedures and plans: written so they can be run and maintained, not just submitted.
