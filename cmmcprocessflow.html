The mature substrate and today's federal content flow into a single seam — three artifacts the Department builds once and publishes — which defines the evidence a supplier's existing tooling already emits. How to read it: nothing left of the seam is proposed — it is federally built or designated and shipping today. The three orange nodes are the entire net-new lift, and they are published content, not a system to procure. Everything right of the CBOM is consumption of one signed artifact. Whatever the mapping cannot reach falls to Tier 3, which is exactly where scarce assessor hours should go. Draft — component versions and the mapping coverage pending verification.

Left

Substrate + federal content

OSCAL, SCAP, and vuln data underneath; 800-171/171A, ODP values, the OSCAL catalog, NCP checklists, hardened images, and the CMVP/FedRAMP registries on top. All public, all maintained.

Middle

The seam — the only new work

An OSCAL profile of the ODP values, a policy designation of which objectives count and at what tier, and the settings-to-objectives mapping. Together they define the CBOM spec. Built once, inherited by all.

Right

One artifact, many readers

Existing supplier tooling emits a signed OSCAL CBOM; DoW, prime, and assessor each verify it independently. The unmappable residual is Tier 3 — human judgment on boundary, architecture, and CUI flow.
